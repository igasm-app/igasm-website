After your period

igasm, by Venex Labs. Last updated 4 November 2026.

The days after a period, when many people notice desire building, and plenty notice no change at all.

Written by the igasm team from the studies and health services cited on this page. Not yet reviewed by a clinician: a named reviewer and a review date will appear here before this page is listed in search.

Phases are estimated from period dates, and only about 13% of real cycles run 28 days (Bull et al. 2019), so read this as gentle context for how you feel, never as a schedule.

Many notice desire building now

Research finds that for many people desire tends to climb in the days after a period, toward mid-cycle. Plenty notice no change at all. Your own log is what counts here.

Moderate evidence · Source: Roney and Simmons 2013

Say one thing you want

Couples who talk about sex well tend to be more satisfied, and quality matters more than quantity. Many start with one small wish, shared outside the bedroom, with no pressure to act on it.

Strong evidence · Source: Meta-analysis, 93 studies

Lube is not a verdict

Feeling turned on and the body's physical response do not always line up. In a large study, people using lubricant reported more pleasure and fewer genital symptoms. Water- or silicone-based is fine with latex condoms.

Moderate evidence · Source: Herbenick et al. 2011

Bleeding after sex? Get checked

Spotting or bleeding after sex is often minor, but health services advise getting any unusual bleeding checked by a GP or sexual health clinic. It is a quick, routine visit.

Strong evidence · Source: NHS
