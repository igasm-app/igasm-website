Before your period

The days before a period: a common dip in desire, lighter sleep, tender breasts and mood that repeats.

Written by the igasm team from the studies and health services cited on this page. Not yet reviewed by a clinician: a named reviewer and a review date will appear here before this page is listed in search.

Phases are estimated from period dates, and only about 13% of real cycles run 28 days (Bull et al. 2019), so read this as gentle context for how you feel, never as a schedule.

A pre-period dip is common

Research links the hormone shift after mid-cycle with lower desire for many people, though some feel the opposite. A dip is not a relationship problem. Your log shows what is true for you.

Moderate evidence · Source: Jones et al. 2018

Sleep may feel lighter now

Some people sleep less well in the days before a period, and better sleep is linked with more desire the next day. Consider an earlier night before planning anything.

Moderate evidence · Source: Kalmbach et al. 2015

Tender chest? Change the plan

Chest tenderness before a period is common. Say so, skip touch that hurts, and try positions without chest pressure. A lump, redness, or pain that stops daily life is worth a clinician visit.

Moderate evidence · Source: nidirect (NHS)

Low mood that repeats monthly

If low mood, anger, or anxiety return before most periods and clear after, a daily log over two cycles is exactly what clinicians use. If it is hard to live with, ask for help.

Strong evidence · Source: Reilly et al. 2024

Closeness counts, sex optional

Everyday affection, like a long hug or holding hands, is linked with better mood and wellbeing, and seems to carry much of why sex feels good for couples. It still counts on low-desire days.

Moderate evidence · Source: Debrot et al. 2013
