Mid-cycle desire on average, why it is only ever an estimate, and the one-sided twinge some people feel.

Written by the igasm team from the studies and health services cited on this page. Not yet reviewed by a clinician: a named reviewer and a review date will appear here before this page is listed in search.

Phases are estimated from period dates, and only about 13% of real cycles run 28 days (Bull et al. 2019), so read this as gentle context for how you feel, never as a schedule.

Mid-cycle desire often runs higher

On average, studies find desire and sex a little more frequent around mid-cycle, with wide variation between people and between cycles. If you feel it, enjoy it. If you do not, nothing is off.

Moderate evidence · Source: Arslan et al. 2021

A one-sided twinge can happen

About one in five people feel a mild one-sided ache mid-cycle, lasting minutes to a day or two. If it changes, lasts longer, comes with bleeding, or is severe, check with a clinician.

Moderate evidence · Source: MedlinePlus

Estimated, not exact

Only about 13% of real cycles are 28 days, and mid-cycle moves around. igasm estimates phases from your period dates, so treat these labels as gentle context for how you feel, nothing more.

Strong evidence · Source: Bull et al. 2019

Notice who reaches first

In one study the mid-cycle rise showed up only in sex started by the person with the cycle. Logging who initiated helps you see your own desire, separate from a partner's.

Moderate evidence · Source: Bullivant et al. 2004
