On a hormonal method

igasm, by Venex Labs. Last updated 4 November 2026.

On the pill, patch, ring, implant, injection or a hormonal IUD: why cycle labels stop fitting, and what to watch.

Written by the igasm team from the studies and health services cited on this page. Not yet reviewed by a clinician: a named reviewer and a review date will appear here before this page is listed in search.

Your pattern may look flatter

Studies find that cycle-linked shifts in desire are smaller or absent on combined pills, and the pill-break bleed is not a true period. igasm shows your own patterns instead of phase labels.

Moderate evidence · Source: Elaut et al. 2014

Desire changes vary person to person

Most people on the pill notice no change in desire or an increase; a minority notice a dip. If a change bothers you, your prescriber can talk through options with you.

Mixed evidence · Source: Pastor et al. 2013 summary

Mention mood changes early

Large studies link starting hormonal methods with a small rise in mood problems for some people, especially teens. That link is not proven to be the reason. If your mood shifts after starting or switching, tell your prescriber.

Moderate evidence · Source: Skovlund et al. 2016
