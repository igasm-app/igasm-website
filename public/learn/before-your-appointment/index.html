Before your appointment

igasm, by Venex Labs. Last updated 4 November 2026.

A one-page checklist to fill in before you talk to a clinician about sex, pain or your cycle. Print it, or save it as a PDF, and take it with you.

Written by the igasm team from the studies and health services cited on this page. Not yet reviewed by a clinician: a named reviewer and a review date will appear here before this page is listed in search.

Sexual and cycle concerns are common and clinicians hear about them every day. In a large national survey, about 1 in 13 sexually active people with a vagina reported painful sex lasting months (Mitchell et al. 2017). Notes made over time are useful: for symptoms that return before periods, a daily record over two cycles is what clinicians use (Reilly et al. 2024), and guidance for period pain asks clinicians to listen to a person's own account of their symptoms (NICE NG73).

Write down

  • What is happening, in your own words.
  • When it started, and whether it came on slowly or suddenly.
  • How often it happens, and whether it is getting better, worse or staying the same.
  • If there is pain: where it is, what it feels like, and whether it comes during sex, after, or at other times.
  • Whether it follows your cycle: the dates of your last few periods help (the cycle length tool below works them out).
  • Any bleeding that is new for you, such as after sex or between periods.
  • Medicines you take, and any hormonal method or IUD, with when you started or changed it.
  • What makes it easier, and what makes it harder.
  • How it affects your life, your relationship, your sleep or your mood.
  • What you would most like to come away with: an explanation, a test, a referral, or options.

Questions you might ask

  • What could be causing this, and what would help tell those apart?
  • Is there anything I can try at home in the meantime?
  • Should I see someone else, such as a gynaecologist, a sexual health clinic or a pelvic health physiotherapist?
  • When should I come back if it does not get better?

You can bring a partner, a friend or a written list, and you can ask for a different clinician if you would be more comfortable. Informational wellness only. Not medical advice.
