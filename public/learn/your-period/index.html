Your period

igasm, by Venex Labs. Last updated 4 November 2026.

Comfort, protection and pain during a period, and when pain is worth raising with a clinician.

Written by the igasm team from the studies and health services cited on this page. Not yet reviewed by a clinician: a named reviewer and a review date will appear here before this page is listed in search.

Phases are estimated from period dates, and only about 13% of real cycles run 28 days (Bull et al. 2019), so read this as gentle context for how you feel, never as a schedule.

Period sex is your call

There is no general medical reason to avoid sex on your period, and no reason you have to want it. A dark towel, the shower, or outercourse can make it easier. Your comfort decides.

Moderate evidence · Source: Arch Gynecol Obstet review 2010

Protection still matters today

Blood can carry infections, and some studies link period sex with more past STIs. If either of you could have an STI, condoms or dental dams lower the risk, period or not.

Mixed evidence · Source: Tanfer and Aral 1996

Warmth and movement on period days

Some people find warmth on the belly or back, or movement they enjoy, makes period days feel more comfortable. A Cochrane review gathers the studies on movement. Pain that is strong or new is one for a clinician.

Moderate evidence · Source: Cochrane: exercise for period pain

Orgasm on period days

Some people say an orgasm, on your own or together, leaves period days feeling a little easier for a while. That comes from an industry survey by Lunette and Womanizer, not clinical research. Your log shows what you notice.

Mixed evidence · Source: Lunette x Womanizer survey (industry)

When period pain needs a clinician

Pain that stops you doing everyday things, deep pain during or after sex, or bowel or bladder pain that comes with your cycle are worth raising with a clinician. You deserve answers, not endurance.

Strong evidence · Source: NICE NG73

Pregnancy is possible on any day, including during a period. igasm is not a method of contraception or a conception aid and cannot tell you when pregnancy is or is not possible. For contraception or pregnancy planning, talk to a clinician.
